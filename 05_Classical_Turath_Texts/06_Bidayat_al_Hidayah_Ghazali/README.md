# Bidayat al-Hidayah (بداية الهداية لحجة الإسلام الغزالي)
### The Beginning of Guidance
**Author**: Hujjat al-Islam Imam Abu Hamid Muhammad al-Ghazali (450–505 AH / 1058–1111 CE)  
**Corpus Provenance**: Open Islamic Texts Initiative (OpenITI)  
**Discipline**: Tazkiyah (Spiritual Purification), Adab (Islamic Etiquette), and Practical Devotion

---

## Overview & Scope
*Bidayat al-Hidayah* ("The Beginning of Guidance") is Imam al-Ghazali's foundational primer on living a life of conscious devotion (*taqwa*). Imam al-Ghazali describes this book as an introductory manual to his monumental work *Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din* (The Revival of the Religious Sciences), serving as the essential practical step for anyone seeking true spiritual guidance before advancing to deeper metaphysical knowledge.

---

## Major Thematic Divisions

### Part I: On Acts of Obedience (*Fi al-Ta'at*)
A comprehensive daily liturgical and devotional regimen for the seeker:
1. **Waking from Sleep & Morning Remembrance**: The state of heart upon opening one's eyes, supplications of dawn, and gratitude.
2. **Etiquette of the Restroom & Purification**: Ritual cleanliness (*Taharah*) as an outward preparation for inward purity.
3. **The Methodology of Wudu (Ablution)**: The physical actions paired with the spiritual realities and prayers associated with washing each limb.
4. **Ghusl (Ritual Bathing)**: Obligations, sunan, and spiritual mindfulness.
5. **Leaving for the Mosque**: Walking with tranquility, dignity, and supplication.
6. **Entering the Mosque & Voluntary Prayers**: Tahiyyat al-Masjid, Duha prayer, and posture in the presence of the Lord.
7. **The Congregational Prayer (Salat al-Jama'ah)**: The role of the Imam, following in unison, spiritual presence (*khushu'*), and adherence to the Sunnah.
8. **The Period between Sunrise and Sunset**: Structuring time into four constructive pursuits:
   - Seeking beneficial knowledge (*'Ilm nafi'*).
   - Engaging in continuous worship, Quran recitation, and tasbih.
   - Serving fellow believers, attending funerals, and visiting the sick.
   - Earning a lawful livelihood (*kasb halal*) with justice and integrity.
9. **Preparation for Sleep**: Examining one's conscience before resting, repentance (*tawbah*), and sleeping in a state of ritual purity as if departing this life.

### Part II: On Refraining from Disobedience (*Tark al-Ma'asi*)
Guarding the seven primary physical limbs through which spiritual ruin enters:
1. **The Eyes**: Guarding against unlawful sights, looking with contempt at others, or prying into faults.
2. **The Ears**: Avoiding backbiting (*ghibah*), slander, falsehood, and foul speech.
3. **The Tongue**: Abstaining from eight destructive verbal vices: lying, breaking promises, backbiting, arguing/disputation, self-praise, cursing, praying against creatures, and mocking.
4. **The Stomach**: Abstaining from unlawful food (*haram*) and doubtful sustenance (*shubuhat*), and observing moderation.
5. **The Private Parts**: Guarding chastity and purity in intention and deed.
6. **The Hands**: Preventing them from striking, harming, or writing what is unlawful.
7. **The Feet**: Preventing them from walking toward oppression or disobedience.

### Part III: Guarding the Heart (*Amrad al-Qalb*)
Uprooting the three root spiritual poisons:
1. **Envy** (*Hasad*): Resenting the blessings bestowed by Allah upon others.
2. **Ostentation** (*Riya'*): Seeking praise and status among humans through religious deeds.
3. **Conceit & Arrogance** (*'Ujb & Kibr*): Viewing oneself as superior to any creature of Allah.

### Part IV: The Etiquette of Companionship (*Adab al-Suhbah*)
- Etiquette with the Creator (mindfulness, awe, humility).
- Etiquette of the student with the scholar.
- Etiquette of the scholar with students.
- Etiquette with parents, family, neighbors, and fellow Muslims.
