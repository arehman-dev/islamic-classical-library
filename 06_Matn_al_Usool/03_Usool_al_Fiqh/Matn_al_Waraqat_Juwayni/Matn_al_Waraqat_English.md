# Matn al-Waraqat fi Usul al-Fiqh (متن الورقات)
### By Imam al-Haramayn al-Juwayni (419–478 AH)
*The Universal Classical Primer on the Principles and Methodology of Islamic Legal Theory*

---

### 1. Definition of Usul al-Fiqh
These are short pages containing the principles of jurisprudence (*Usul al-Fiqh*).
- The term *Usul al-Fiqh* is composed of two parts: *Usul* (roots/principles) and *Fiqh* (jurisprudence).
- An **Asl** (foundation/principle) is that upon which something else is built.
- A **Far'** (branch) is that which is built upon something else.
- **Fiqh** is: *Knowledge of the practical legal rulings attained through detailed textual evidence.*

---

### 2. The Seven Legal Rulings (Al-Ahkam al-Shar'iyyah)
The legal rulings are seven:
1. **Wajib** (Obligatory): That for which the doer is rewarded and the neglecter deserves punishment.
2. **Mandub** (Recommended): That for which the doer is rewarded but the neglecter is not punished.
3. **Mubah** (Permissible): That in which neither performance nor omission incurs praise or blame.
4. **Mahzur / Haram** (Prohibited): That for which the abstainer is rewarded (out of obedience) and the doer deserves punishment.
5. **Makruh** (Disliked): That for which the abstainer is rewarded but the doer is not punished.
6. **Sahih** (Valid): That which complies with the law and has legal effect.
7. **Batil** (Void/Invalid): That which fails to comply with the law and produces no legal effect.

---

### 3. Sources of Islamic Law
The primary sources and linguistic methods of deduction in Usul al-Fiqh comprise:
1. The Holy **Qur'an** (الكتاب)
2. The Prophetic **Sunnah** (السنة)
3. Scholarly Consensus (**Al-Ijma'** - الإجماع)
4. Legal Analogy (**Al-Qiyas** - القياس)
5. Linguistic expressions: General (*'Amm*) and Specific (*Khass*), Absolute (*Mutlaq*) and Qualified (*Muqayyad*), Commands (*Amr*) and Prohibitions (*Nahy*).
6. Presumption of Original Continuity (**Al-Istishab** - الاستصحاب).
7. Independent Juristic Reasoning (**Al-Ijtihad** - الاجتهاد).
